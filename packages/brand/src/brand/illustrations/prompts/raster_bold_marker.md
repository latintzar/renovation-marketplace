# STYLE (fixed — same for every image)

Matisse drew this in thirty seconds with a black felt-tip marker, with a child's freedom and a painter's instinct for gesture. Naive but alive — not a child's generic schema (round head, stick legs), not an illustrator's careful draftsmanship. The drawing captures the ESSENCE of the thing through pose, weight, and distortion: a panther stalks, a bottle leans with weight, a B bulges on its belly. Perspective bends to serve the feeling. Details are few and chosen — one eye-dot placed just so carries more than ten hairs would.

**Vibe:** cool, confident-casual, dry. Think of the illustration register of modern design-forward brands — Babbo, Graza, Fly By Jing, Omsom in food; Teenage Engineering, Nothing, Playdate in hardware. Deliberate under-polish. Minimal ornament. No cuteness, no twee, no "quirky", no wide innocent eyes. An adult drew this, fast, and it shows taste. If a detail doesn't carry the gesture, leave it out.

**The line, every time:**
- ONE black marker, ONE medium width. Same thickness at the start of a stroke, middle of a stroke, end of a stroke. Same thickness across every drawing — a panther and a letter B in this style have identical stroke weight.
- Committed: every line is drawn once, with conviction, and kept. No careful sketching, no erasing, no fussing, no re-tracing into a manic scribble. A line that went wrong stays wrong — that's the charm.
- Wobbly and imperfect: corners overshoot, closures miss, curves bulge, straights bow. The hand trembles because it's moving fast, not because it's unsure. Marker on rough paper — solid black ink with a softly fuzzy edge.
- Whimsical and abstract: a few simple shapes standing in for the real thing. Bend the subject to make it alive. A bottle can lean. A panther's back can arc impossibly. A letter can lean drunk. If a strict proportion kills the feeling, break the proportion.
- ESPECIALLY for animals and people: do NOT draw them anatomically or observationally. Abstract the creature to its iconic silhouette and a few gesture lines — think Matisse cutout, Picasso one-line animal, Haring figure, a mascot on a natural-wine label. A panther is a long curved back + prowling stance + tail flick, not a correctly-proportioned cat. A bird is a swoop with a beak. A person is a pose. One eye-dot. One snout line. The gesture IS the animal.

**Forbidden:**
- No colour. No grey. No shading. No hatching. No cross-hatching. No gradients. Black ink only, pure white background.
- No thicks-and-thins, no brush-taper, no weight hierarchy.
- No anatomical correctness, no observational draftsmanship, no clipart smoothness, no flat-icon geometry, no generative cute line-art polish.
- No stick-figure childishness either — no round-head-with-two-dots-and-stick-legs generic schemas. We're Matisse-naive, not kid-naive.
- No manic multi-pass scribbling.

# FRAMING (fixed)

- 1:1 square canvas. Pure white background.
- ONE motif only. Never two things. Never "X with Y". Never scenes.
- Motif fills 40–65% of the frame. Centred. Fully visible — nothing cropped, nothing bleeding off the edge. Generous empty white margin on all sides.
